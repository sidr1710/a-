## 6. Why people watch, share and act (Prompt 6)

| Beat | Emotion | Trigger | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| Approaching a stranger by a supercar | **Curiosity + social risk** | "Will he answer?" | Curiosity gap (Loewenstein): an open question the viewer needs closed |
| "What do you do for a living?" | **Voyeurism / status curiosity** | Asking about money, which is taboo | Social comparison: we measure ourselves against others' status |
| The reveal: a boring business or unknown billionaire | **Surprise → hope** | "I could do that" | Unknown people go viral more than celebrities [V: H&H: Tom Cruise got 13M, an unknown consultant 106M] |
| The number | **Aspiration** | A specific money figure | Specific numbers seem more believable and are easy to repeat |
| The lesson / faith / regret | **Meaning, validation** | "Believes all his wealth belongs to God", "Was it worth it?" | Reframes success; viewers share it to signal their own values |
| The ending | **Share / save** | A quotable line | People share to show their identity ("send this to a friend who…") |

**The core emotional promise [I]:** *"You're one conversation away from the life these people have, and here's the conversation."* The viewer wants to become someone with **options and respect**.

**What makes people share [I]:** signalling values (faith, hard work), friendly arguments ("prenups", "marriage was my biggest mistake": 987K [V]), and advice they want someone else to see.

**The downside, which you should avoid [V+I]:**
- Presenting arranged interviews as spontaneous leads to "is it staged?" doubts.
- Flaunting wealth and focusing on money can feed envy, and it skews toward survivorship bias (you only hear from the winners).
- **In India this is sharper [I]:** people are more private about income, flaunting draws tax and security worries, and envy content triggers backlash.
- **Your values-led version:** swap "How much do you make?" for "What did it cost you to build this?" and "How is AI changing your business?". You keep the curiosity without exposing their wealth.

## 7. How content turns into relationships (Prompt 7)

**How it works [V: H&H]:**
1. **Give value first:** "All these people coming on calls, I've already posted them and gotten them millions of views."
2. **Borrowed trust moves along a chain:** one famous guest opens the door to the next ("once we interviewed one major entrepreneur, it became easier to attract others").
3. **Referrals come from anywhere:** a woman in Beverly Hills → her brother (Speechify, $3B) → Scooter Braun, **in one day**.
4. **Keep going:** it took a year to get Tom Brady until someone they knew on his team helped. Mark Cuban (in the host's story) finally replied on the third email.
5. **An interview is a low-risk first meeting:** busy people turn down cold DMs but agree to a 5-minute interview that gives them visibility and a legacy.
6. **Why guests say yes [V]:** "Legacy. They're able to give back." Plus attention: "two of the biggest currencies are money and attention."

### Your 7-step content-led networking system
1. **Pick 3 locations and 1 theme** a month (e.g. Worli Sea Face + Lower Parel + BKC × "AI in family businesses").
2. **Line up 5 guests in advance** through LinkedIn, alumni networks and warm introductions, plus **5 cold approaches** on the shoot day.
3. **Film, publish within 72 hours, and send the guest the clip file** plus a caption they can post. Guests reshare, and you get their audience.
4. **Day 7: send a thank-you and the view count.** Offer a free 30-minute "AI audit" of their business. This is not a pitch; it's a gift.
5. **Day 30: invite them to host one live session** for your community (15 minutes of Q&A). They get more visibility, you get a draw for the session.
6. **Always ask:** "Who's one person you respect whom I should interview next?" This creates the referral chain.
7. **Quarterly: host an offline "Builders Dinner" in Worli or Lower Parel** for past guests. That turns individual relationships into a network, which becomes your moat.

**Outreach scripts:**
- **LinkedIn / DM (pre-arranged):** "Hi [Name] — I run aiwithsid, a series on how Mumbai's business builders think about AI and legacy. We're filming at [Worli/Lower Parel] on [date]. 10 minutes, standing, 6 questions, and you get the edited clip to post. Would you be open to it?"
- **Street (cold):** "Excuse me sir — 30 seconds? We're asking Mumbai's business owners one question: how is AI changing your business?" If they hesitate: "No income questions. Just your one best lesson."
- **Follow-up (day 7):** "Your clip crossed [X] views 🙏 As a thank-you, we'd like to do a free 30-minute AI audit of your operations: three things you could automate this quarter. Worth a coffee this week?"

### 12.4 Red team: sceptical investor + veteran Indian creator
**Five most likely reasons it fails:**
1. **Mumbai's wealthy won't talk on camera.** They're more private than Americans. *Fix:* no income questions, lead with AI and lessons, and aim for 70% arranged guests at first.
2. **Too many offers too early** (community + courses + webinar + agency). *Fix:* for 90 days, run only **content → AI audit → agency**, plus a monthly free session.
3. **Weak first 3 seconds.** English street content competes with Hindi entertainment. *Fix:* test hooks aggressively and keep the quality bar.
4. **The founder's time runs out** (2 people shooting, editing, selling and delivering). *Fix:* batch shoots, AI-assisted editing, one editor as the first hire once agency revenue allows.
5. **Trust gaps** (unverified testimonials, contradictions on the page). *Fix:* the landing page audit items first.

**Assumptions not yet verified:** Mumbai guest willingness · price tolerance for ₹999–1,999/mo · agency pricing · whether English-only limits reach (test one Hinglish variant).

1. Book 5 arranged guests for one Worli / Lower Parel shoot day.
