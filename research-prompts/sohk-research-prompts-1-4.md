## PROMPT 2: How they make money

### Attention → Trust → Revenue
```
Street and celebrity interviews (200–250M views/month, 5 platforms)
      │  borrowed authority: "access to the top 1%"
      ▼
Trust and brand equity (they turn down brands that would dilute it, e.g. gambling)
      │
      ├──► Platform payouts: "Facebook pays me more than YT, IG and TikTok combined"
      ├──► Brand sponsorships: "a couple million bucks this year"
      ├──► School of Mentors, $49/mo × ~6,000 members
      ├──► School of Content: application → sales call (price [U])
      ├──► Done-for-you agency: ~$10K/month per client
      └──► Speaking $30–50K per keynote · newsletter · Amazon affiliate links
      │
      ▼
NETWORK: every guest who got millions of views returns the favour by appearing
on member calls for free, referring the next guest, and becoming an agency client
```

### Revenue streams
| Stream | Evidence | Price | Est. share of revenue | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **School of Mentors** (subscription) | "close to 6,000" members [V: H&H]; Skool shows 6.2K | **$49/mo** (was $99), 7-day free trial | 6,000 × $49 ≈ **$294K/mo ≈ 20% of $1.5M** [I] | Medium |
| **Done-for-you agency** | "Built into a multimillion-dollar business" [V: H&H] | "~$10,000 a month" [V: H&H]* | Likely the biggest single stream: e.g. 40–60 clients ≈ $400–600K/mo [I] | Low–Medium |
| **School of Content** (coaching) | Application page with a VSL (video sales letter) [V: schoolofscaling.io] | [U], probably high-ticket | [U] | Low |

| **Platform payouts** | Facebook is their biggest payer [V: H&H] | – | Supports the business but isn't it [I] | Medium |
| **Speaking** | Gotham Artists listing [V] | $30–50K per event | Small [I] | Medium |
| Newsletter ads, Amazon affiliate | Links exist [V] | – | Small [I] | Low |

*He said "$10,000 a month for the consultation" right after describing the done-for-you agency, so it's unclear which product that price belongs to. Treat it as the price level for their top offer.

**Which stream is the real business?** [I] **The agency and the community.** The media brand exists to (a) find agency clients (wealthy guests who want a personal brand), (b) attract community members, and (c) sell sponsorships. James said it directly: *"Who are we spending the most time with? Super wealthy entrepreneurs. So let's start a marketing agency where we build their personal brands."*

### Their business logic, in their own words [V: H&H]
1. **"Get to the root of why people follow you."** People follow for *access to the top 1%*, so they sell access (School of Mentors).
2. **"Who's making money off your efforts?"** The guests benefit from the exposure, so they sell those guests content services (the agency). Their term for this is "vertical integration".
3. **Poll your audience before you launch a product.** "Spend a couple weeks, a month… polling your audience exactly what they want."
4. **Never depend on platform payouts.** The Facebook strike cut their income by more than 80% overnight.
5. **Reinvest.** The co-founders paid themselves about $2K a month while earning $30K a month.
6. **Protect brand equity.** They did one gambling sponsorship (PrizePicks), saw the audience reaction, and never did another.
7. **Hold a quality bar.** "The last video I posted on Instagram under 1M views was April 2024." They won't post a paid interview unless they believe it can reach 1M views.

### Estimated economics of one interview [I, all assumptions]
- **Cost:** host plus a 2-person crew, travel (James took 150 flights last year), and a few hours of editing. Roughly $1–3K per interview including travel, or much less when several are filmed in one city on the same day.
- **Value:** an average of ~10M views [V: VUE] × roughly $2–5 CPM from platform payouts (mostly Facebook) gives ~$5–15K directly. The real value is the **guest relationship** (a possible agency client, a free mentor call, referrals to other guests) and the **sponsorship inventory**.
- **Conclusion:** the direct return per video is modest. The network return is very large.

### What could break it
Losing a platform (it already happened once), James being the single face of the brand (burnout, 150 flights a year), copycats (he says "a whole bunch of people trying to do the exact same thing"), and scepticism about staged interviews. He says about half are arranged in advance and half are genuinely cold approaches. [V: H&H]

### Pricing psychology
- **Price compared with something relatable:** "Access… for the price of a couple sub sandwiches a month" compared with a "$100K mastermind" (they name YPO). [V: H&H]
- **Risk removed:** a 7-day free trial, "just cancel, no questions asked". The Skool page shows tenure badges like "Still a paying member after 18 months".
- **Price cut from $99 to $49:** more volume, and they make up the revenue on the higher tiers [I].
- **A disclaimer on the high-ticket offer:** "We don't believe in get-rich-quick programs… we can not and do not make any guarantees" (on the School of Content application page). **Copy this for your India pages.**
- **Credibility argument:** "I've done 5 billion views for myself. I know this stuff", which positions them against coaches who haven't done what they teach.

**Where the profit is** [I]: the $49 community brings in a large volume of buyers and keeps guests engaged as mentors. The **agency (~$10K/mo per client)** and School of Content are where most of the profit comes from.

### Leaks we found
- **The link to jointheschoolofmentors.com/home2 in their YouTube About section now redirects to a gambling spam site.** It looks like the domain expired or was hijacked (checked 25 Sep 2026). Even an 8-figure brand loses visitors through dead links. **Check your own links every month.**

## What this means for you (aiwithsid / Growth Brothers, Mumbai)

1. **Their 2021 pivot solves your current problem.** Your page positions *you* as the teacher, like SOHK in its first year (stuck at 25K followers). **Point the camera at Mumbai's successful business owners** and let *their* credibility build yours: "Excuse me sir, what does your business do? How are you using AI?"
2. **Your agency matches their agency.** Every Worli, BKC or Lower Parel business owner you interview is a potential Growth Brothers client. After the video gets views, offer a free "AI audit" and follow up. That's their vertical integration applied to you.
3. **Their community model works in India too.** They charge $49/mo for weekly calls with guests. Yours could be ₹999–1,999/mo for weekly calls with the Mumbai founders you've interviewed. Only launch it after you have ~20 guests and have asked your audience whether they'd pay.
4. **Their faith angle supports yours.** James openly says "aside from God, nobody's coming to save you" and appears on *Faith Driven Entrepreneur*. Values-led content works at scale, so your AI × Gita angle is a strength if the content leads with guests and results.
5. **Their free big-name event → trial is the model for your webinar.** "Free live session with [known Mumbai founder]" will convert much better than "free masterclass with me".
6. **Protect brand equity from the start.** Turn down gambling, trading-tip and "get rich" sponsors. Add their style of earnings disclaimer.
7. **Hold a quality bar.** They skip videos they don't think can reach 1M views. You can set your own threshold, e.g. don't post unless the first 3 seconds would stop a stranger scrolling.
